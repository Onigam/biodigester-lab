# The 3D of the Biodigester Lab

State of the four worlds, written by `lab-modeler`, kit 0.6.0, against research §0.4 and §4, physics §9 and K6's correction (physics §7). What each is, how it is built, what is tested, what it costs, what remains. `docs/scenery.md` and `docs/water.md` hold the setting and the slurry. **Not a build plan:** no cut line, no torque, no procedure in any of it, and nothing here shows that a build would be safe or allowed.

**Wired in `src/lab.ts`.** What follows was measured on a review page of the modeler's, the kit's real frame around the real worlds with stub sheets, which the lead removed on wiring.

## 1. The machine: one function, every world

`machine/machine.ts`, `buildMachine`, builds the two tanks and their fittings from `dims.ts` in the machine's own frame: origin at the tank's inner floor on its axis, +Y up, +X the feed, -X the overflow, -Z the back. A world places the root and nothing else. `yard` and `gas-line` use it closed, unregistered, as the same one mesh set. `digester` registers its twenty parts. `update` is the one place that reads the simulation: the bell, the levels, the layers, the bubbles, the brick, the bucket. A test holds fourteen shared parts within 3 mm across the three worlds at one state.

**Three views, all geometry, swapped by `setView`, no clipping plane at draw time.** `machine/clip.ts` cuts each closed solid once, when the world is built, and closes its cut with a real face (`cutGeometry`, tested for area on a wall, a pipe cut along and a pipe cut across). So nothing z-fights, nothing needs a stencil, and a click goes where the eye goes.

| View     | When                                                                                                                                                                   | What it is                                                                                                                           |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `cut`    | `digester`, at rest                                                                                                                                                    | Along the axis, in the plane of the feed and the overflow. It is also the plane the bell tilts in, so the cut stays true as it moves |
| `across` | `digester`, the section tool, and `rim-gap` selected                                                                                                                   | The plane of the overflow, from above: the open ring, 22 % of the surface. The bell there is a live collar, as the plane cuts it     |
| `whole`  | `yard`, `gas-line`; `digester` under x-ray or isolate. **A guide's step stays `cut`** (lead, on wiring: the inner feed pipe of step 4 cannot be seen in a closed tank) | The closed machine                                                                                                                   |

**The section tool cuts it the other way, it does not open it,** because the world opens cut: research calls it « the machine, in section ». That is the solar lab's `tank-and-tap` choice. From the standard camera the ring is seen at a low angle; orbit up. **For the lead:** `onCutaway` could fly the camera up (§7).

**Selecting `rim-gap` asks for that section by itself.** Nowhere else can the ring be seen: under its bell in the cut, 1 to 3 % of it shows from every side (QA M2). `src/main.ts` hands the stage to `stage-link.ts`, as the solar lab's does, since the kit tells a world nothing of the selection (kit request 14). It gives way when flows are on, since the voice's grey dots run along the cut, and when the part is isolated, exploded or under x-ray. The button « Coupe » does not light up for it. A voice's `[[cutaway:off]]` while the part is selected therefore keeps the section: `part-rim-gap` goes from the section to the dots and back to the section, on the words about the ring.

**One machine drawn, whatever the size in Simulation (QA B2).** `asDrawn` in `machine.ts` carries a state of another size into the drawn tank by shares: the bell by its share of travel, the surface at the drawn overflow and pressed down as far as the model's pressure presses it, the layers by their share of the depth. On the drawn machine the state passes through untouched. The panel's hint and `scene-digester` both say the drawing stays the 1,000 L tank: a drawing that followed would need every machine's proportions, cuts, hose, framings and labels, and the audio would have to change. Figures and the gauge keep the chosen machine's numbers, so the marks on the bell then read its share, not its litres.

**Part boxes stay whole.** A cut part keeps an invisible two-point marker of its whole box, so the sheet's « Encombrement » and a framing are the whole part's, whichever half is drawn. Tested on the tank.

### Tilt, and what it costs

The bell leans from the middle of what it has in the tank (`bellPose`), its high side towards the feed. A test walks it through the travel: clear of the bore at both ends, hoops counted; at rest its high side on the pipe within 3 mm; at full travel its top clears the limiter's bars by 2.1 cm at most. The cut's plane contains the tilt, which is why the section is along the feed and not across it.

### Details worth knowing

- **The scale** is on the back of the bell, from the section's left edge round to the back, so the home view sees it at a slant past the cut and `scale-marks` has its own `frame` and `viewDir`. Read against the tank's rim. In `gas-line` the machine is turned, so it faces the viewer. **Its figures run down the bearings:** from outside the bell a rising bearing goes to the viewer's left, and running up them mirrored every figure (QA M9). A test reads them from outside.
- **The brick** is on the pad beside the pallet at `addedWeight` 0, on the bell above 0, a second one above 18 kg. The state has no key for the weight: `update` takes it from the parameters, so the worlds take `params`.
- **The gauge** reads `burnerMbar` while the flame burns, `pressureMbar` otherwise, on its real 0 to 60 mbar: the needle barely leaves zero, as it must have on the measured plant. A sheet can say so.
- **The relief** is a clear bottle: the water in its dip tube stands lower than the bottle's by a centimetre per millibar, the model's. **Its 8 cm of depth is an estimate.** The model has no relief.
- **The trap:** its water is `trapWaterMl`. With `trap` off the jar goes and `hoseWaterMl` stands in the hose as a plug at the low point, as long on each side as the bore says. **Corrected by the lead on wiring:** the trap's brass was baked into the part and stayed in the air, the plug was a straight bar across the air, and with no water the hose had a gap. The body is `dynamic` now, a short loop joins the hose where the jar was, the plug follows the hose. A test holds all three.
- **The flame:** tongues on the ports, height from `flameShare`, which physics §6 says is not a measured height. Shown only while `flameOn`, or when the part is alone. **Its part has nothing to outline when the model says off:** the author can give it a « light the burner » action (`sim.lightBurner()`).
- **Flows:** the digester's gas lost at the rim shows dots in proportion to `bubblesRimLph`; the line's gas runs at the state's true speed in the bore (about 0.6 m/s) on `view.slowMo`, and stands still along its way while the burner is out, as the gas does; the feed, the liquor and the yard's run at a speed for the eye, stated nowhere as true. A dot keeps a minimum size as a share of the picture, about 9.5 px in a 678 px view (`FLOW_MIN`; `FLOW_FINE` in the cut tank), and a cap close up, a bead in a darker ring of its colour. Share of the view that changes by more than 40/255 at home, flows on against off: `yard` 0.44 %, `gas-line` 0.54, `digester` 0.89, `slurry` 2.51, the same to a hundredth in the dark theme, whose worlds stay on paper.
- **Labels at 768 and 390 px.** Cut, overlapping or under the panel at each home: 0 at 1280, 768 and 390, in both languages, by the review's script. Five labels have a short name, the sheets keep theirs: « Cuisine », `Kitchen`, « Brûleur », `Burner`, « Particule », « Piège à eau », « Tuyau ». A test lists them. In a narrow view the line's overview leaves the burner's name room (`WorldRegistry`, kit request 15). **With the phone's sheet raised they hold in `yard` and `gas-line`, and not in `digester` (11 overlaps) or `slurry` (3 and 4): `docs/qa.md`, the fix round.**
- **Under x-ray or a guide's wire, no leaf card** in the yard: the kit's ghost has no alpha (kit request 16), and thousands stacked into blue clouds (QA M4). The tree's wood and the beds' planks and soil keep the ghost; a selected tree or bed keeps its leaves.

## 3. Attached and resting

Tested, `machine/machine.test.ts` and `worlds.test.ts`: tank on the pallet, pallet on the pad, pad on the ground, within 3 mm; the bell's high side on the inner pipe at rest; funnel on riser on tee on stub on connector on wall on pipe, gaps under 1 mm; plug, overflow, bottom valve, limiter on what carries them; the tap on the bell at every height; the brick flat on the bell or on the pad; the bucket on the pad, under the overflow's end, its handle hanging clear of it; the hose on the tap at every height in all three worlds, on its sleeve at the wall; pot on the stand's fingers, stand and burner on the table, trap and buckets on the floor, vent and detector on the wall; everything standing on the yard's ground. The hose's tube is rebuilt only when the tap has moved.

**Isolate:** scenery goes when one part is alone: pad, hose, bench, board, walls, the yard's setting, tested. In the drop the drop stays, on purpose: a kind alone in its liquid. **Exploded:** the bell lifts with what it carries, the slurry slides out of the cut, layer over layer, the feed comes off along its own line stage by stage, the pallet stays and the tank lifts off it, so that nothing goes through the pad. The hose and the bucket go, and the bubbles stop, while anything is apart. **In `gas-line` the machine stays,** closed, with the hose on its tap: it is not a part there, the hose does not move, and hidden it left the hose hanging from nothing and blinked out at each of the assembly's steps 15 to 20 (QA B1).
