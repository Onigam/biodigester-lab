## 0. Summary and the plan against the bar

### 0.1 The machine

**A household floating-bell digester made of two plastic water tanks: a 1,000-litre tank holding about 850 litres of slurry, and a 750-litre tank turned upside down inside it, which floats on the slurry and rises as gas collects.** Fed 2 kg of kitchen scraps and 18 litres of water a day, at about 30 °C, one such plant was measured for four months in Dar es Salaam in 2008 (S5). Cooked leftovers gave 289 L of gas a day at 57 % methane, peelings and market waste 126 L at 66 %. A mix of the two, 200 L, ran the plant's small burner for **45 minutes**, a third of an average household's daily cooking there.

This is the ARTI compact biogas plant, an Indian design (Dr A. D. Karve, Ashden Award 2006). Why this one (Q1): it is the only small digester for kitchen scraps for which I could read **a drawing with dimensions, a parts list, a four-month independent measurement of gas, composition, pH and burn time, and a survey of what happened to the plants in the field**. It also has the bell the roadmap asks for, and it answers the film's question directly. The lab draws a **reconstruction of that plant**: no logo, the design named in the Sources panel. It is an independent educational reconstruction, not a plan to build.

### 0.2 What the check of the sources changed

1. **The roadmap's two videos do not point to this machine, and neither may show a bell.** The Low-tech Lab's written tutorial for its 2018 film (S3) is a **60-litre drum with a 150-litre flexible bag**, no bell, heated at 38 °C by a bed-warmer mat. Its other wiki page (S4) is a **9 m³ pit with a tarpaulin holder and bricks**. I could not get the transcripts (§1.7), so what the films themselves show is **unread**. The roadmap's device list (floating bell, condensate trap, water seal) is therefore not one machine in any source I read. I keep the bell (the roadmap's « aha ») and say which parts come from where (§0.5, Q6).
2. **A licence, stated plainly (§1.2).** The 2022 Low-tech Lab video is tagged **Creative Commons Attribution** on YouTube. The 2018 one carries a non-standard label, **« CC-BY-NC-ND-SA »**: no clip from it. The wiki pages are **CC BY**. By the lab's own rule no footage is reused anyway; these are credited and linked.
3. **Wet or dry: the figure that misleads.** The designer's headline, « 2 kg of feed gives 500 g of methane », is for **dry matter** (S10, S11). Two kilos of real wet scraps gave **about 105 g** (derived from S5, K20). Almost a factor of five. Same scraps, same plant.
4. **« 1 kg of waste = half an hour of gas » (S3) is true on one condition.** On the Dar es Salaam burner, cooked leftovers give 32 minutes per kilo, but peelings 14 (derived, K21). At the 0.4 m³/h of an average biogas stove (S8) and the book's rule of thumb of 100 L/kg, it is 15 minutes. **Half an hour of what flame?** About 1.5 kW going in (derived, K24), small by French standards.
5. **Half a brick really doubles the pressure, and the arithmetic retrieves it.** 12 kg on 0.58 m² is 2.03 mbar, and S5 read 2 → 4 mbar (K18). It is the cleanest exact test in the lab and a good image. The flow, however, rose 1.84 times where a plain orifice gives 1.41 (§2.8, conflict 8). Reported, not explained.
6. **The honest core: a warm-climate machine, and most of them stopped.** In October 2008, of 12 household plants visited, **4 were running**, and 10 after five weeks of repairs (K30). One stopped plant read **pH 4.15** (K31). The designer says it works « above 30 °C » (S10), Eawag that below a mean 15 °C it « does not work satisfactorily » (S8). Air in Geneva averages 1.7 °C in January, 10.9 °C over the year (K47). By hand, in a 10 °C month a straw-wrapped plant would spend most of its gas keeping itself warm, and **the cold dilution water alone takes about a third** (§2.9). The lab says so plainly, and the French or Swiss visitor is the first to whom it applies.
7. **It leaks by design.** The bell floats directly in the slurry, so gas rising in the open ring is lost: **22 % of the surface** by area (S5), **about 17.5 % measured** on larger plants of the same kind (S7). A review warns of losses up to 40 % across small digesters and a climate break-even anywhere from 3 to 51 % (S27, summary only). By my hand estimate the rim alone can outweigh the LPG it replaces (§2.9). A lab that sells a green flame without this would not be honest (lesson 7).
8. **Safety sources disagree, and some reassure too much.** S5 says desulphurisation is « not necessary », no explosion was reported in Tanzania, and calls methane « toxic ». Yet its own instrument read **H₂S above 100 ppm, its ceiling, on nearly every plant**, and H₂S dulls the sense of smell from about 100 ppm (S15, S18). Explosive ranges quoted for biogas differ: 6–12 % (S5, S8), 10–24 % (S15). None of this reaches a visitor as reassurance (§7).
9. **The makers of the gas are not bacteria.** S3 says « bacteria », as most sources do. Methanogens are the organisms that led to the third domain of life (S31). The lab and the film say it correctly: this is also an opening (§10).

### 0.5 The two guides

- **Assembly (20): how it goes together.** The order follows the build manual's contents (S9), with the Tanzanian parts list (S5 Table 13). **Installation (10): from the place to the daily feed**, reported by S5: sun, pad, water beside it, inoculum, ten days without feed, the ramp, the daily routine, the checks.
- **Not a build plan.** BRIEF §8 applies: biogas is a named risky topic. Said once at the top of each guide: this is a reading of published work for learning, not a procedure to follow, and nothing here shows that a build would be safe or allowed. No cut line, no torque, no tool list as instruction. Dimensions appear because a source publishes them, marked in the sheets, and the sources stay linked with credit.
- **Every step ends on its origin** (_reported_, _usual practice_, _our reading_), as in the sand and solar labs; a data test can check it.
- **Composite parts are named as such (Q6).** The Dar es Salaam plant had a tap, a hose and a stove. The trap, relief, filter and arrester come from S3 and S8, and from the roadmap's list. The float limiter comes from S9.
- **The mechanics still hold:** the bell is carried by what it floats on, the hose moves with it, every id of `digester` and `gas-line` is installed in exactly one step.

### 0.6 Sheets, lessons, spoken guide

**Sheets (A3, from `dims.ts`, estimates marked):** 1 `general-arrangement` (the yard, plan and elevation) · 2 `digester-section` (official and estimated dimensions told apart) · 3 `bell-and-rim` (areas, the scale, weight and pressure, the open ring) · 4 `feed-and-overflow` (levels at empty and full bell) · 5 `gas-line-schematic` (what the measured plant had, what others add) · 6 `food-chain` (the four steps, with what each makes) · 7 `mass-balance` (2 kg + 18 L in; gas, effluent, sludge out, model output) · 8 `warmth-and-time` (rate against temperature, a year in four climates, heat budget) · 9 `hazards` (the gas's figures side by side, where each collects, no promise) · 10 `size-comparison` (60-litre drum, this plant, 1,500/1,000, 4,000/3,000, to scale, each with its feed and gas).

**Lessons (two questions each):** 1 `what-makes-the-gas` (hydrolysis to methane; why no air; methanogens are not bacteria) · 2 `wet-or-dry` (TS and VS; why 2 kg gives 500 g or 105 g; why leftovers beat peelings per kilo but not per kilo of solids) · 3 `the-bell` (a floating lid is a store and a regulator; the brick; the scale; why the flame may die with gas left, S7) · 4 `warmth-and-time` (first-order decay, Arrhenius, residence time, four climates, the cold water; can the gas pay for its own heat?) · 5 `feed-or-sour` (**the failure**: overfeed, acid outruns methanogens, pH falls, and gas stops when you feed more. How S5 went to 5 kg safely by ramping; the pH 4.15 plant) · 6 `what-the-gas-can-do` (air mix, H₂S and the nose that stops smelling, no oxygen in a tank, CO from a bad flame, germs in the effluent) · 7 `is-it-worth-it` (leaks and the climate, what it replaces, the money, and the 4 of 12).

**Spoken guide:** 58 parts × about 270 characters, star parts (`gas-bell`, `methanogens`, `blue-flame`) at about 400, four worlds, an introduction: about 22,000 characters per language. Character to follow: **one grain of rice** that falls into the funnel and comes out as a blue flame, in the Pelton manner. Anonymous as usual. Said once: reconstruction, model figures, a warning where the gesture is risky. Written after the checkpoint, from the final sheets. Local voice (`ROADMAP` §10).

### 2.8 Conflicts between sources, and what the lab does

1. **Bell size.** S5 Table 13: Ø1.30, 0.90 high. Fig. 7: Ø0.90, 1.30 high. A Ø1.30 × 0.90 tank holds 1.19 m³, and could not enter a Ø1.03 tank. **I take Fig. 7**, supported by K4 (r = 0.455 m) and K5 (inner r = 0.43 m).
2. **Wet and dry.** ARTI's 2 kg → 500 g is dry; S5's 2 kg is wet (K36, K20). The lab shows both on one bar.
3. **« A day or two » against 42.5 days.** Not the same thing: a reaction time for starch, a residence time for the liquid, and in this unstirred tank a solids time near 300 days. Nobody I read measured the designer's speed on scraps. K26 hints that gas follows feed within days. The model finds what it finds.
4. **Half an hour per kilo:** §0.2 point 4. The lab gives minutes per kilo with its burner stated.
5. **22 % or 17.5 %.** One is an area on the 1,000/750, not a gas measurement. The other is a measured loss on 4,000/3,000 plants. **Important: every yield in S5 is gas caught in the bell, without the rim.** The model has one parameter, default 22 %, slider 0–30 %.
6. **Explosive range of biogas.** 6–12 % (Deublein, in S5 and S8). 10–24 % measured at INERIS for 50 % CH₄ / 50 % CO₂ in saturated air (S15). Methane alone 5–15 % (S15). S5's text applies 6–12 % to CH₄ on p. 13 and to biogas on p. 53. **The lab prints S15's two with their condition.** It never presents any range as a safe side.
7. **H₂S.** S3's 1–3 % is above S8's maximum of 2 %. K32 cannot settle it: the sensor stopped at 100 ppm. The lab quotes S8.
8. **Pressure and flow.** Doubling the pressure raised the flow 1.84 times (269 → 496 L/h, derived); an orifice gives 1.41. The gauge read to 60 mbar and was read at 2 and 4, so it may be coarse, and the gas's flame changes with it. The physicist reports the gap.
9. **259 or 234 NL/d.** Table 20 and p. 37 use different periods. K19 keeps Table 20.
10. **Small slips in S5:** the abstract's « 27.9 mg/l (MW) » for effluent NH₄-N is the influent, the text says 85.5. 92.2 % and 92.5 % VS, 84.9 % and 86.1 % TS, by different averages. The SGP on p. 37 has broken units. S6's 290 and 130 L/d are rounded (K19).
11. **Is methane « toxic »?** S5 p. 53 says methane and CO₂ are « toxic ». S15 classes them as causing **anoxia**. The lab follows S15.

## 5. Physics, for `lab-physicist`

**New module `sim/bio`** (roadmap), with `sim/thermal` from the kit for the tank's temperature. No DOM. **Never tuned to K19 to K26**: the model reports its numbers beside them. All kinetic parameters are estimates with wide sliders.

### 5.1 The biology

Minimum that shows the failure: particulate VS in two pools (fast, slow), first order with k(T); acids as a pool, made by the acid-makers and eaten by a slow methanogen population; alkalinity holding pH; methanogen rate cut by low pH; washout by flow. The two-population form is what lets « feed more, get less » appear by itself. ADM1 is far too much.

- **Temperature:** Arrhenius, `Ea` slider 40–80 kJ/mol. At 60, k at 15 °C is 0.29 of k at 30 °C, at 20 °C 0.44, at 37 °C 1.71 (arithmetic). **Estimate**; thin clues in §2.10.
- **Yield cap:** food waste K45, 396 L CH₄/kg VS, with S5's 326 NL and 417 NL/kg VS beside it. **Methane share**: `find` from a carbohydrate-protein-fat mix via Buswell, or `input` from K20 at first.
- **Layers:** S5's stratification means the liquid's HRT and solids' SRT differ (42.5 and about 300 days). Treat solids retention as its own parameter. Do not treat this tank as stirred, or it will wash out too easily.

### 5.2 The bell

Pressure = (effective bell weight + added weight) · g / A. `exact` test on the brick (2.03 mbar). V = A · rise. At the top of the travel, gas bubbles out at the rim (S5). Rim loss as a fraction of gas made, by open area (K4), slider. Level in the feed pipe follows pressure: 1 mbar is about 1 cm of water, so 2 mbar lifts the feed-pipe level about 2 cm above the tank's. Compare with the drawn 1.06 and 1.04 m. **That passes on the figure, by hand, to be tested.**

### 5.3 The line and the burner

Hose loss small but real. Burner Q from pressure: fit form Q = C·ΔPⁿ and **report** n against the orifice's 0.5 and the two measured points (conflict 8). Flame needs a minimum pressure: scenario 9, K34. **Condensate:** water at the low point blocks gas in proportion to the slope; a scenario, not a precise model. Water vapour from K43.

### 5.4 Temperature and parameters

One node, slurry mass about 850 kg, with the feed water's enthalpy, walls by UA, the air (K47), sun on a black tank (solar gain). `compare` with S5: digester at 27.8 to 30.9 °C when air averaged 26–28 (K17, K47), a useful check that a black tank in sun runs warmer than the air. Metabolic heat is small; say so.

### 7.2 Law, per country

I am not qualified to classify a household plant, and the lab must not imply it is allowed. It says: **« such an installation is subject to the rules of your country; ask your local authority before you build anything »**, and nothing firmer unless a lawyer checks.

- **France, installations.** ICPE rubric 2781 covers methanisation: 2781-1 (raw plant matter, livestock effluent, stercoral matter, whey, agri-food plant waste), 2781-2 (« autres déchets non dangereux »); above 100 t/d authorisation; 30–100 t/d registration; below 30 t/d declaration for 2781-1 (S21, t, slides of 2022). **I found no lower limit or household exemption in what I read, and I do not know whether a kitchen-scale plant is treated as an ICPE, or what applies to 2781-2 below 30 t/d.** S20 shows what a declared plant must do. It is not a household text, but it is a fair picture of what the state considers normal: an overpressure device, a relief, a gas alarm at 10 % of LEL in rooms with unwelded gas pipes, covered digestate. **The DREAL or prefecture decides. Not determined here.** Gas inside a dwelling: the arrêté of 2 August 1977 (from memory, **not verified**). Separate sorting of biowaste since 1 January 2024, under the AGEC law (from memory, **not verified**).
- **EU, animal by-products: a trap for kitchen scraps.** Catering waste is excluded from Regulation 1069/2009, **except** if destined « for transformation into biogas » (S22). Catering waste is defined to include « household kitchens » (S23). So, read literally, scraps containing animal matter sent to a digester may fall under that regulation, whose default for biogas is pasteurisation at 70 °C for 60 min at 12 mm (S24, s; **Annex V not read**), with room for national rules on catering waste (S24, s). **Whether anyone applies this to a private household I do not know.** Worth one sentence in the lab, and no more, once a person has read Annex V.
- **Switzerland.** A cantonal sheet on food waste cites the same 12 mm, 70 °C, one hour for fermentation (S24, s). Federal rules **not checked**.
- **Germany.** UBA paper (S25, s); TRGS 529 for work at biogas plants (from memory, **not verified**).
- **UK.** Retained EU text (S23); exemptions for small digestion **not checked**.
- **Tanzania, India:** S5 found no specific obstacle in Tanzania in 2008. Not looked up further.
- **Workplace rules** (ATEX 1999/92/EC, French limit values) concern workplaces, not homes. Used as a reference for what the hazard is, never as what a household must do.

## 10. The film

Calendar: 24 December (`PLANNING` §4.2). Title is `video-writer`'s; `fact-checker` verifies. Working question: **« Can kitchen scraps cook your dinner? » / « Des épluchures peuvent-elles cuire votre dîner ? »**

### 10.1 Facts that could open a film or a Short

Each is true as far as the source given, and the wording matters.

1. **« The flame comes from a kind of life nobody knew was its own branch until 1977. »** Woese and Fox's three lines of descent place the methanogens in the group they named the archaebacteria (S31, abstract read). Methanogens themselves were known earlier, as bacteria, so never say they were « discovered in 1977 ». The term « Archaea » came in 1990 (s). _Strength:_ one of the great finds of biology, sitting under a saucepan. **To read: the full paper.**
2. **« Twenty-four years before his battery, Volta was stirring mud in a lake to set fire to what bubbled up. »** November 1776, near Angera on Lake Maggiore; « inflammable air native to marshes » (S32, f). Battery 1800 (general knowledge). The man the volt is named after, in the channel's series on energy. **The stick is from a page seen in a search only: re-read before saying it.**
3. **For a Short, the machine doing something (first-frame rule):** a black drum rising alone from a bigger drum, nothing touching it, fed on lunch. Then half a brick placed on it: the flame doubles. **Measured:** 17 cm per 100 L; 2 → 4 mbar; 100 L burn in 22 minutes, then 12 (K5, K18, K22). The flame's height at each pressure was not measured: the picture comes from the model.
4. **The honest turn in the film:** of 12 plants visited, **4 running** (K30). Say it with the second half: 10 after five weeks of repairs, October–November 2008. Why they stopped is the story: a pipe, a blockage, too much food, no one to ask.

### 10.2 Traps for the script

- « 2 kg → 500 g of methane » is **dry matter** (K36). « 1 kg = half an hour » needs a named burner and leftovers (§0.2). « 45 minutes » is **a thesis's calculation from two substrates**, a third of a Tanzanian household's 2.5 h on charcoal, about **1.5 kW**: not a French dinner.
- **Not bacteria alone:** the methane comes from archaea. **Not « free energy »**, not « zero waste », not « carbon neutral » (§2.9, S27). **Not clean water out:** COD above 1,000 mg/L, germs survive (S8). **Not odourless**, and **a smell is no alarm**. **No line implying it is safe, or legal at home.**
- **Do not use:** the Bombay leper asylum of 1859 and the Exeter street lamps of 1895. Both circulate in popular histories citing each other; I found no primary record, and the lamps look like a mix of Cameron's septic tank with Webb's separate sewer-gas lamp patent (S37, s). The Assyrians and Persians of other web pages: nothing found, not looked for further. « 42 million household digesters in China » is **installed** by 2015 (S30, s), not running.
- **Temperature:** nothing measured below about 25 °C; France in the film is a simulation, and must be called one.
